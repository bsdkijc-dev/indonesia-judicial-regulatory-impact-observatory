# Ex-Post Regulatory Impact Assessment (RIA) Methodology — IJRIO v1.0

## 1. Purpose
This methodology operationalizes an independent, evidence-based ex-post evaluation of *Peraturan Mahkamah Agung Republik Indonesia* (PERMA) across ten analytical dimensions (A–J).

## 2. Ten Evaluation Dimensions
- **A. Regulatory Problem**: Pre-regulation baseline problem, establishing evidence, affected groups, and problem definition adequacy.
- **B. Regulatory Objectives**: Explicit objectives, SMART specificity, established indicators, and assigned implementation responsibilities.
- **C. Legal Coherence**: Vertical consistency with superior legislation (UUD 1945, UU, PP), horizontal overlaps, contradictions/gaps, and subsequent statutory shifts (e.g., UU 1/2023 KUHP, UU 20/2025 KUHAP).
- **D. Regulatory Design**: Clarity of provisions, allocation of responsibilities, proportionality of procedures, and adequacy of enforcement mechanisms.
- **E. Implementation Effectiveness**: Actual adoption, availability of implementing guidance (SK KMA/SEMA/Juknis), institutional resources, and regional/court-tier disparities.
- **F. Regulatory Efficiency**: Reduction in procedural complexity, cost savings, administrative efficiency, and proportionality of compliance costs to benefits.
- **G. Access to Justice**: Accessibility gains, reduction of procedural barriers, consideration of vulnerable groups, and any newly created barriers.
- **H. Stakeholder Impact**: Differential impacts on Judges, Court Employees, Advocates, Litigants, Legal Aid Organisations (OBH/Posbakum), Government Agencies, Businesses/UMKM, and other groups.
- **I. Unintended Consequences**: New procedural bottlenecks, cost transfers, inequalities, or unexpected institutional burdens.
- **J. Regulatory Sustainability**: Continued relevance, need for amendment, consolidation, replacement, or repeal.

## 3. Causal Inference Guardrails
- Prioritise quasi-experimental or structured comparative designs (before-and-after, interrupted time series, difference-in-differences, qualitative process tracing) where data permit.
- Explicitly test alternative explanations (other regulatory changes, court resource shifts, case composition, external shocks, measurement limits).
- Never claim causation without adequate research design; report descriptive trends or associations otherwise.
