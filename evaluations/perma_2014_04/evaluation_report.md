# Ex-Post Regulatory Impact Evaluation: PERMA No. 4 Tahun 2014 — Juvenile Diversion in Child Criminal Justice

* **PERMA ID**: `PERMA_2014_04`
* **Official Source**: [PERMA 4/2014 (BPK RI)](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/196318/perma-no-4-tahun-2014) (effective `2014-07-24`)
* **Assessment Date**: 2026-11-24
* **Authorship & Governance**: AI-generated analytical draft (`PENDING_HUMAN_LEGAL_REVIEW`); not an official position of Mahkamah Agung RI.

## 1. Regulatory Profile
Evaluates `PERMA_2014_04` (*Pedoman Pelaksanaan Diversi dalam Sistem Peradilan Pidana Anak*, promulgated 2014-07-24), operationalizing court-stage juvenile diversion under `UU No. 11 Tahun 2012` (SPPA).

## 2. Problem and Objective Analysis
Grounded in `regulatory_problems.csv` and `regulatory_objectives.csv`: `UU 11/2012` mandated diversion for juvenile offenses punishable by under 7 years imprisonment (non-recidivist, [PN Palopo](https://pn-palopo.go.id/30-berita/artikel/363-sekilas-tentang-sistem-peradilan-pidana-anak)); `PERMA 4/2014` standardized juvenile-judge diversion conferences (*musyawarah diversi*) and *penetapan*.

## 3. Legal Coherence Assessment
Cross-checked against `legal_coherence.csv` (`COH_027`): consistent with `UU 11/2012` and `PP 65/2015`, while [Jurnal Hukum STIH-PGL (2026)](https://journal.stih-pgl.ac.id/ojs-stih/index.php/jhd/article/download/139/116) notes the need to monitor alignment with the new KUHP (`UU 1/2023`) and KUHAP (`UU 20/2025`).

## 4. Implementation Assessment
Synthesizes Level 2 and Level 3 evidence: juvenile diversion completion is tracked as a formal court KPI (e.g., [PN Kayuagung LKJIP 2025](https://pn-kayuagung.go.id/images/dokumen/LKJIP%20FIXED%20SETELAH%20REVISI.pdf), noting mediation failures when parents/victims disagree), while [AJSH](https://jurnal.ardenjaya.com/index.php/ajsh/article/download/1356/870) reveals major identity-protection gaps in case documentation.

## 5. Impact Assessment
Evaluates empirical indicators in `impact_indicators.csv`: in `AJSH`'s audit of **18 diversion case files**, **12 files (66.7%)** disclosed the child's full name and address, **3 files (16.7%)** included school-uniform photos, and only **2 files (11.1%)** showed active identity protection, while **72% of 48 online news articles** used stigmatizing labels.

## 6. Stakeholder Analysis
Summarizes impacts in `stakeholder_impacts.csv`: diversion protects eligible youth from detention and criminal records and engages Bapas and social workers, but identity leaks caused severe social stigma, school dropout, and relocation among interviewed children (`AJSH`).

## 7. Evidence Quality Assessment
Applies IJRIO Levels 1–4: Level 1 text, Level 2 PN LKJIP 2025/SPPA portals, and Level 3 empirical file/interview audit (`AJSH`) are `VERIFIED`; `D05 — Institutional efficiency` is marked `NOT ASSESSED` due to absent national diversion-turnaround series.

## 9. Policy Recommendations (Analytical Option)
* **Recommended Option**: **OPTION B — RETAIN WITH IMPLEMENTATION IMPROVEMENT**
* **Rationale**: `PERMA 4/2014` remains the core court-stage juvenile diversion framework, but requires mandatory automated/checklist identity redaction across SIPP and Direktori Putusan uploads (addressing the 66.7% name/address exposure rate in `AJSH`), child-friendly facilitator training, and synchronization with `UU 1/2023`, `UU 20/2025`, and `PERMA 1/2024`.

## 10. Research Limitations
1. National juvenile-diversion success rates across all PNs are not yet aggregated in a single open dataset.
2. Quantitative diversion-conference duration and detention-cost savings remain uncompiled.
3. Legal interpretation and final policy classification await human legal review (`PENDING_HUMAN_LEGAL_REVIEW`).
